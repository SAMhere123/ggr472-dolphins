About the 7 different dolphin species in Hawaii:

Common Bottlenose Dolphin (Tursiops truncatus)

Common bottlenose dolphins are medium-sized, robust dolphins recognized by their short, well‑defined rostrum and curved dorsal fin. In Hawaiian waters, they occur as both a large offshore form and a smaller, island‑associated form, with adults typically ranging from 2.5–3.5 m in length (Baird, 2016). Their coloration is gray with a darker cape and lighter belly, and older individuals often accumulate pale linear scars from social interactions and cookie‑cutter shark bites. They live in fission‑fusion societies, forming fluid groups that change composition throughout the day. Around the Hawaiian Islands, they show strong site fidelity, with genetically distinct populations around Kauaʻi/Niʻihau, Oʻahu, Maui Nui, and Hawaiʻi Island (Baird, 2016).
