Current Conservation Measures and Efforts to Safeguard Hawaiian Dolphins

Current conservation efforts for Hawaiian dolphins focus on reducing human-driven threats, protecting critical habitats, and improving long-term population monitoring.

The National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS) has designated several island-associated dolphin populations, such as bottlenose and pantropical spotted dolphins, as distinct management stocks, allowing impacts to be assessed at an appropriate local scale (Baird, 2016). Regulations restricting gillnet use around Maui and parts of O'ahu aim to reduce nearshore entanglement risk for species like bottlenose dolphins, which are especially vulnerable due to their coastal habits (Baird, 2016). To address chronic disturbance of spinner dolphins, the United States National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) has proposed and implemented management strategies, including time-area restrictions and approach limits, to reduce the intensity of tourism-related interactions in key resting bays (Tyne et al., 2018).

Research organizations such as Cascadia Research Collective , and their collaborators at Hawai'i Wildlife Fund , contribute to conservation by conducting long-term photo-identification, satellite tagging, and genetic sampling programs that help identify population structure, track movements, and document human impacts (Baird, 2016). These scientific efforts have also informed legal actions that resulted in restrictions on Navy mid-frequency active sonar in biologically important areas, reducing acoustic disturbance for sensitive dolphin species (Baird, 2016). Together, these measures represent a growing, multi-layered approach to safeguarding Hawaiian dolphins through regulation, research, and habitat-specific management.
